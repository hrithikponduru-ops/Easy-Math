1 — The whole idea

A limit is a destination,
not an arrival.

If someone films you driving toward a petrol station and the film cuts out one metre before you arrive, they can still say with total confidence: you were going to the petrol station. That confident answer is the limit.

How to read it out loud

limx→1  f(x) = 2

"As x sneaks up on 1, f(x) heads for 2."

The arrow → means "sneaks up on", never "equals". That one word is 80% of understanding limits.

Why anyone bothers

Some questions are illegal to ask directly. 0 ÷ 0. "Speed at one exact instant" (no time passes, so no distance — 0/0 again). "Area of a circle" out of straight lines.

Limits are the legal way to ask an illegal question: don't land on the bad spot, just walk up to it and see where things were headed. Every derivative and every integral is built out of this trick.

2 — The famous example

A hole you can never fill

Take f(x) = (x² − 1) / (x − 1). At x = 1 it breaks: top is 0, bottom is 0. The graph has a hole punched in it. But walk up to the hole from either side…

from the left: x = 0.90000 → f(x) = 1.90000
from the right: x = 1.10000 → f(x) = 2.10000
at x = 1 exactly: 0/0 — undefined

Both sides agree: 2

3 — When there's no answer

Two sides must agree

A limit exists only if the left-hand walk and the right-hand walk arrive at the same place. Here are the two ways it goes wrong.

Case 1

The jump

left-hand limit = 1  ≠  right-hand limit = 3
so the limit does not exist

Like a taxi fare that's ₹50 up to 2 km and ₹90 after. Walk toward the 2 km mark from below and above and you disagree about the price. No single destination, no limit.

Case 2

The blow-up

f(x) = 1/x as x → 0
left side dives to −∞, right side rockets to +∞
no single number — no limit

"Infinity" isn't a destination you can arrive at, it's a description of running away. Saying the limit is ∞ is shorthand for "this grows without ever settling".

4 — Limits in your actual day

Three you've already used

Pizza → π

Straight lines become a circle

sides: 3
area of shape: 1.299
heading toward π = 3.14159…

Slice a pizza into more and more pieces and rearrange them: the wobbly edges straighten out. The area of a circle is the limit of shapes made of straight lines. No polygon is ever a circle. The limit is.

Speedometer

Speed at one instant

time window: 1.000 s
average speed over it: 45.0 m/s
heading toward 30.0 m/s

Your speedometer shows speed "right now" — but speed needs two moments to compare. So it shrinks the window toward zero and reports where the average was heading. That's a limit, on your dashboard.

Coffee

Cooling to room temperature

after 0 min
temperature: 90.0°C
heading toward 22° — never quite there

Your coffee is technically always a hair warmer than the room, forever. "It cooled down" is a statement about a limit, not about an arrival.

Four more, in one line each

  • Phone charging. Fast to 80%, crawls to 100% — the charge curve is approaching a ceiling.
  • Skydiver. Falling speed climbs, then flattens at terminal velocity: a limit as time grows.
  • Loading bar at 99%. Progress estimates that halve the remaining time feel eternal for exactly this reason.
  • Compound interest. Split a year into more and more compounding periods and the payout heads for a fixed number: e = 2.718…
5 — Doing it on paper

Plug in first. Panic never.

Find limx→1 (x² − 1)/(x − 1). Tap through:

limx→1 (x² − 1)/(x − 1)

Step 0 — always just try plugging in

Most limits are secretly easy: put the number in and see what happens. Only if that breaks do you do real work.

(1 − 1)/(1 − 1) = 0/0

Step 1 — you got 0/0. Don't panic.

0/0 is not an answer — it's a question. It means "there's a hidden cancellation, go find it." Any answer at all is still possible.

x² − 1 = (x − 1)(x + 1)

Step 2 — factor the troublemaker

The top is a difference of squares. Now the same (x − 1) appears on the top and the bottom — that's the hidden cancellation.

(x−1)(x+1) / (x−1)  =  x + 1

Step 3 — cancel it (this is legal!)

You can only cancel (x−1) if it isn't zero. And it isn't — because x → 1 means x approaches 1 and never equals it. The whole rescue depends on that one word.

1 + 1 = 2

Step 4 — now plug in

The simplified version behaves perfectly at x = 1. Substitute and you're done.

limx→1 (x²−1)/(x−1) = 2

Step 5 — the answer, and what it means

The function still has a hole at x = 1. It has no value there and never will. But it was clearly heading for 2, and that's what a limit reports.

1 / 6

⚠ The three mistakes everyone makes

0/0  ≠  0   ·   0/0  ≠  1   ·   limx→a f(x)  ≠  f(a)

1. Writing "0/0 = 0" or "= 1". It's neither. It's a signal to factor, cancel, or rationalise.

2. Assuming a limit fails just because the function is undefined there. That's the normal, interesting case.

3. Forgetting to check both sides at a jump. One side is only half an answer.

6 — The grown-up definition

A game of "how close do you want?"

Textbooks write this with Greek letters and it terrifies people. It's just a bet between two players.

you: "get f(x) within ±0.50 of 2"
me: "then keep x within ±0.50 of 1"
I win every round → the limit is 2

The bet, in plain words

You name any precision you like — "I want the output within a hundredth of 2." As small as you want. You're trying to catch me out.

I must reply with a zone around x = 1: "stay inside this and I promise you'll get it."

If I can always answer, no matter how mean your demand, then 2 genuinely is the limit. If there's even one demand I can't meet, it isn't.

for every ε > 0
there is a δ > 0 such that
0 < |x − 1| < δ  ⟹  |f(x) − 2| < ε

That's the same sentence in symbols. ε is your demand, δ is my zone, and 0 < |x − 1| is the fine print that says you never actually stand on x = 1 — the hole is excluded on purpose.

8 — Keep only this

The five-line version

  • A limit is where a function is heading, not where it lands.
  • The value at the point is irrelevant. Hole, jump, anything — the limit ignores it.
  • Both sides must agree, or there's no limit.
  • Try substituting first. If it works, that's the answer.
  • 0/0 means "factor and cancel", not "give up".
limx→a f(x) = L   —   "as x sneaks up on a, f(x) heads for L"
Limits are the floor the whole building stands on: a derivative is a limit of slopes, an integral is a limit of sums. Get "heading toward" and the rest is bookkeeping.
