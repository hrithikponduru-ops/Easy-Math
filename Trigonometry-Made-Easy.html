Trig, but
actually easy

Sine, cosine, tangent and cotangent are just ways to compare the sides of a right triangle. That is the whole big secret.

Show me the triangle ↓

First: meet the only triangle we need.

A right triangle has one square corner: exactly 90°.

Pick the angle you care about, called θ (“theta”). Then name the three sides from that angle.

Important: opposite and adjacent can switch when you choose a different angle. The hypotenuse never switches.

Four tiny recipes

A trig “function” is just a division recipe. Divide one side length by another.

Sine

SIN

opposite ÷ hypotenuse

How much the triangle goes up compared with its longest side.

Cosine

COS

adjacent ÷ hypotenuse

How much the triangle goes across compared with its longest side.

Tangent

TAN

opposite ÷ adjacent

How steep it is: rise divided by run.

Cotangent

COT

adjacent ÷ opposite

Tangent flipped upside down. So cot = 1 ÷ tan.

SOH: Sin = Opp/Hyp   CAH: Cos = Adj/Hyp   TOA: Tan = Opp/Adj

Move the angle. Watch everything change.

The bottom side stays 10 units long. Drag the slider and the triangle grows taller.

Small angle = gentle slope. Big angle = steep slope.

Notice: as the angle grows, tan grows fast. That is why tangent is great for measuring steepness.

Where would I use this?

Anywhere an angle, a distance, and a height meet.

SIN

Ladder safety

You know the ladder length and its angle. Use sin to find how high it reaches.

height = ladder × sin(angle)

TAN

Tree from its shadow

Measure the shadow and the sun angle. Use tan to find the tree height.

height = shadow × tan(angle)

TAN / COS

Roofs and ramps

Builders use tan for slope and cos to turn a slanted beam length into horizontal distance.

Useful for safe ramps and roofs that drain rain.

SIN + COS

Maps and navigation

Split a diagonal trip into north/south and east/west parts.

vertical = distance × sin(angle)
horizontal = distance × cos(angle)

Tiny quiz

You know the side beside the angle and want the side across from it. Which ratio connects opposite and adjacent?

Pick one. Hint: remember TOA.

The two-step way to solve almost any beginner problem

  1. Circle what you know and what you want.
  2. Pick the recipe containing those two sides: SOH, CAH, or TOA.

Example: adjacent = 10, angle = 30°, want opposite.
tan(30°) = opposite ÷ 10
So opposite = 10 × tan(30°) ≈ 5.77.

Keep this cheat sheet

Need height from a slanted length?
Try sine: opposite = hypotenuse × sin(angle).
Need horizontal from a slanted length?
Try cosine: adjacent = hypotenuse × cos(angle).
Need height from ground distance?
Try tangent: opposite = adjacent × tan(angle).
Need ground distance from height?
Try cotangent: adjacent = opposite × cot(angle).
